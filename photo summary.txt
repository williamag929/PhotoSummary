User Story: A Day in the Life of Frank
The Old Way (Before SiteScribe):

It’s 3:00 PM on a Tuesday. Frank is walking the third floor of a new office building project. The electricians are running conduit. Frank notices that the spacing on the conduit supports looks too wide.

He pulls out his phone with one hand and a crumpled blueprint with the other. He balances the large sheet against a wall, trying to find the electrical specifications page. The wind blows it shut. He gets frustrated, pulls out a tape measure, and confirms the supports are 12 feet apart, not the required 10 feet.

Now the documentation begins.

He puts the blueprint away.

He takes a photo with his phone's camera. The photo is just called IMG_4583.JPG.

He opens a separate notes app and tries to type with his dusty thumbs: "elec conduit suport spcing 3rd flr. looks wide. check spec E-301."

Later that night, at 7 PM, Frank is at his kitchen table creating his daily report. He sees IMG_4583.JPG and his cryptic note. He has to remember the exact location, what the measurement was, and what the correct specification was. It takes him 10 minutes just for this one issue. He has 15 other issues to document from the day.

The New Way (With SiteScribe):

It’s 3:00 PM on a Wednesday. Frank is walking the fourth floor. He sees a similar issue with the conduit supports. This time, he just pulls out his phone and opens the SiteScribe app.

Step 1: Listen to the Specs
He walks over to the area. He has a single Bluetooth earbud in. He taps the "Electrical" module in the app for his current project. He says to the app's voice assistant:

Frank: "Hey SiteScribe, pull up specs for conduit supports."

SiteScribe (audio in his earbud): "Specification E-301, section 4.2 states: 'Conduit supports for 3/4 inch EMT must be spaced at a maximum of 10 feet on center.'"

Frank didn't have to look at a blueprint. He instantly knows the requirement. He pulls out his tape measure and confirms the supports are 12 feet apart.

Step 2: Photo and Voice Memo
He taps the camera icon within the "Electrical" module. He frames the shot to clearly show the conduit, the support, and his tape measure showing the 12-foot span. As he holds the phone up, he taps the microphone icon on the screen.

Frank (speaking to his phone): "Non-conformance issue. Location is fourth floor, northeast quadrant, above grid line C. Conduit supports are spaced at twelve feet. Specification E-301 requires ten-foot spacing. Tagging 'Sparks Electrical' subcontractor. This needs to be remediated before the ceiling grid is installed on Friday. End entry."

SiteScribe uses voice-to-text to instantly transcribe his words and permanently associate the text, location, subcontractor tag, and priority with the photo he just took. The entry is automatically saved and categorized under "Electrical Issues."

Step 3: Generate the Final Report
At 4:45 PM, Frank is back in the site trailer, packing up for the day. He opens SiteScribe, navigates to the "Reports" section, and taps "Create Daily Report."

He selects the date. The app shows him all 12 entries he created that day, each with a photo and a clear description. He reviews them for a moment and taps "Generate Final Report."

Within seconds, the app produces a professional PDF document.

Header: BuildRight Construction - Daily Site Report - Project 1042 - [Date]

Summary Text: A bulleted list of the day's key issues and action items.

Electrical: 1 Non-Conformance Issue (Conduit Spacing) assigned to Sparks Electrical.

Plumbing: 2 Progress Photos (Riser installation complete).

Safety: 1 Observation (Water on floor near stairwell) - Marked as resolved.

Detailed Entries: Each photo is displayed clearly, followed by:

Photo: [The picture Frank took of the conduit]

Timestamp: 3:05 PM

Location: Fourth Floor, Northeast Quadrant, Grid Line C

Details: "Non-conformance issue. Conduit supports are spaced at twelve feet. Specification E-301 requires ten-foot spacing."

Action Required: "Assigned to: Sparks Electrical. Needs to be remediated before the ceiling grid is installed on Friday."

With one more tap, Frank emails the report to the Project Manager, the client's representative, and the foreman for Sparks Electrical.

He closes his phone, packs his bag, and leaves the job site at 5:01 PM. His reporting is already done, it's perfectly accurate, and everyone who needs to know has been notified.
